# Elf

Elves were once a single people. Long ago — before the current age, in a time the histories of younger species do not reach — they fractured. Three elven peoples now walk Aldermark, each shaped by the path their ancestors took.

Some elves alive today were children in the old age. They will not say so to humans, who would not believe them.

\---

## Wood Elves

Wood Elves remained closest to the world they always knew. They live in forests across Aldermark — small woodland enclaves, sometimes a few families, sometimes a few hundred. They worship the **Fey Mother**, an old name for an older being whose true name is no longer spoken aloud. Their ties to the Feywild run deep, and some say their holy places drift between this world and that one when no mortal eye is watching.

Wood Elves are fierce protectors of their forests but not isolationists. They trade with nearby villages, hire on as scouts and trackers, and are often the first called when a community is troubled by a monstrous creature in the wilds. A Wood Elf at a village market is a known thing — uncommon, but not strange.

They keep tales of past ages, but the tales are oral, and even the elves are no longer certain which parts of them happened.

### Adventuring Hooks

* A grove or wild place under threat
* Something the Fey Mother is asking of you that the elders cannot interpret
* A debt of hospitality to a village that once helped your enclave
* A monster from the old tales has been seen again

## High Elves

High Elves are the rarest of the three. In ages forgotten, they sought out dragons and bargained for their secrets — and so became the first arcane spellcasters of the world. They still speak Draconic, still keep the company of dragons where dragons can still be found, and they revere their ancient patrons as something between gods and ancestors.

Most humans in Aldermark have never met a High Elf and many believe they are simply a story. Those who know better know that High Elves know things — about magic, about dragons, about the past age — that no one else does.

A High Elf does not usually pass through Aldermark by accident. If one is here, they are looking for something.

### Adventuring Hooks

* A search for a dragon, a relic, or a fragment of lost arcane knowledge
* An errand from your patron — a dragon whose patience is not infinite
* Exile from your enclave for an unforgivable mistake
* Something has gone wrong with a working older than the current age

## Drow

The Drow come from far away — vast deserts and dense jungles in places the people of Aldermark have only heard rumors of. Their culture is alien to this region, and their physical appearance — pale hair, eyes adapted to absolute darkness, and dark skin adapted to withstand a cruel sun — is unlike anything most Aldermark folk have ever seen.

A Drow walking into a small village often clears the streets. Doors lock. Children are pulled inside. In a keep with armed guards, the reaction is closer to wary curiosity. They are not assumed to be powerful in the way an aasimar might be — they are assumed to be *strange*, in a region where strangeness is rarely safe.

A Drow in Aldermark is, by definition, far from home. Whether they came willingly or not, and whether they want to go back, are usually the first two questions worth answering.

### Adventuring Hooks

* Exiled, fleeing, or hunted from your homeland
* A diplomatic or trade errand from a faraway power that no one here has heard of
* Searching for someone — or something — that came north before you did
* A choice to be the first of your people Aldermark ever sees as a friend
