# Holmlette

Holmlette is a small farming village of perhaps two hundred souls, set on the western edge of the Aldwud. It sits within the protectorate of Aldwud Keep, half a day's ride to the north, and pays its dues in grain, timber, and pelts.

It is not a remarkable village. It has a stone church, a wooden inn, a smithy, a mill on the small river that runs through it, and a stretch of fenced fields that have fed the same families for generations. The dirt road that runs through the village center continues north to the Keep and east into the trees, where it is mostly used by woodcutters who do not go far in.

Holmlette is rarely troubled by other holds. It is too small, too poor, and too quiet to interest its neighbors. What threats it faces come from the Aldwud — wolves in winter, the occasional bear, more rarely a beast of stranger description. The villagers know to stay clear of the deeper wood, and the deeper wood, mostly, has been content to stay clear of them.

That has been changing.

## People of Note

These are the names a Holmlette native would recognize:

**Reeve Aldric** — Holmlette's reeve, appointed by Lord Tolwen to manage the village in his name. A practical man in his fifties, gray at the temples, more competent than charismatic. Knows everyone's business and most of their debts.

**Father Beldwin** — The village priest, tending the small stone church at the village center. Honest and pious, but a country cleric — his theology is simpler than a city scholar's, and his answers to hard questions tend to be "we trust the gods."

**Old Maeve** — The village's herbalist and wise woman. Lives in a cottage at the village's south edge with too many cats. Knows which leaves treat a fever, which mushrooms kill a man, and most of the older stories no one else bothers to remember. Children love her; some of their parents are not sure how they feel.

**Gertha** — Owner of the Holmlette Inn, the village's only gathering hall. Loud, quick-tempered, generous with portions, and the unofficial center of village gossip. Knows what every traveler said about every other traveler going back a decade.

**Tomas** — The village smith. Quiet, broad-shouldered, drinks more than he should. His brother went into the Aldwud last summer and did not come back. He has not been the same since.

**Wynna** — A young huntress who works the edges of the Aldwud, providing the village with venison, hides, and word of what she sees out there. The closest thing Holmlette has to a forest scout. She has been worried lately, and saying so.

## Daily Life

Holmlette runs on the seasons. Spring and autumn are planting and harvest. Summer is the long work of weeding, herding, and trading at the larger market a day's walk west. Winter is huddled around hearths, telling stories, and praying the snowfall doesn't break the roof. The Aldwud provides timber, game, and herbs. The river provides fish and water for the mill.

The village owns one priest, one smithy, one inn, one mill, one dozen militia (in name; in practice perhaps four men who can hold a spear and one sergeant who can shout), and a great deal of patience.

## Recent Troubles

In the past few weeks, things have begun going wrong:

- **The water has soured.** Buckets drawn from certain wells now smell faintly off. A child fell ill after drinking from the river; a goat died after the same. Father Beldwin has blessed the wells but the trouble has not stopped.
- **The animals have grown strange.** Hunters report deer that did not flee, and once a fox that did not seem to see them at all. Wynna saw a wolf with sores on its flanks that should have killed it and hadn't.
- **People have gone missing.** Two woodcutters and a young girl who was gathering mushrooms at the wood's edge. None have returned.

Reeve Aldric is worried. Father Beldwin has begun praying nightly. Old Maeve has stopped saying anything about it at all, which some find more troubling than if she had spoken up.

There is a feeling, in Holmlette, that something is wrong in the Aldwud. No one yet knows what.
