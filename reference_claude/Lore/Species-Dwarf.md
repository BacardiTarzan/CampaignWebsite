# Dwarf

The dwarves of Aldermark hail from a single great mountain kingdom in the north — a labyrinth of halls, forges, and vaults beneath peaks that no other people have mapped. The kingdom is unified in name only. In practice it is a fractious arrangement of clans, each with their own hold, history, and political ambition. Clan rivalry shapes dwarven life as deeply as stone shapes their cities.

Dwarven memory is long, and dwarven grudges longer. Their loremasters keep records of an older age, when dwarven kingdoms covered far more than the northern range — kingdoms now lost, remembered only in songs about the **Forsaken Halls**.

Outside the mountain, dwarves are uncommon but welcome. Their craftsmanship is without equal — a dwarven blade, hinge, lock, or hearth-stone is treasured wherever it is found — and most communities are glad to host one. Dwarves who leave the mountain are typically those with adventuring spirits, those caught on the wrong side of clan politics, or those pursuing a grudge that has crossed generations.

## The Dwarven Pantheon

Dwarves worship a wide pantheon — most clans claim their own patrons, ancestors, and holy dead — but two gods are recognized in every hold:

* **Odmar the All-Forger** — father-god, lawgiver, maker. The first hammer, the first hearth, the keeper of oaths.
* **Kangerdaggin** — the war-god of grudges, defender of the deep halls, oath-bringer of vengeance.

Lesser gods of mining, brewing, smithing, ancestors, and the honored dead number in the dozens. Different clans elevate different patrons, and these differences feed many of the clan rivalries.

## On Goliaths

Dwarves do not let go of the goliath wars. For ages, dwarves and goliaths fought over mountain territory — though the goliaths' true masters were the giants who enslaved them. Even today, when a goliath has clearly broken free and shows no love for their old overlords, most dwarves regard them with cool suspicion at best. Individual dwarves can be friendlier; entire clans, almost never.

## Adventuring Hooks

* Clan exile or political fallout
* A personal grudge that crosses generations
* A craft commission requiring rare materials from beyond the mountain
* A relic lost to a Forsaken Hall, calling you home
