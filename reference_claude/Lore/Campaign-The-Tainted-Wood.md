# Campaign Brief: The Tainted Wood

*DM reference. Not for player eyes.*

## The Surface Mystery

Holmlette and the surrounding edge of the Aldwud are suffering a slow corruption — tainted water, sickened wildlife, missing villagers, a wrongness in the trees. The villagers know something is wrong but do not know what, and most assume it's some kind of natural blight or beast.

## The Deep Truth

In the deep Aldwud, there is a temple. It is older than the current age — built when celestial beings still walked Aldermark openly, dedicated to an Upper-Planes power whose name has been lost. After the past age ended, the temple was buried, forgotten, and overgrown. Its hallowed protections persisted, in a faded form, holding back things that should not enter the Material Plane easily.

Someone has found the temple.

That someone has spent months desecrating its hallowed ground and using it, inverted, as a focus to summon a demon from the Lower Planes. The summoning is not yet complete. The corruption Holmlette is suffering is *bleed* — the protections of the temple, weakening, leaking outward as the desecration progresses. The animals are crazed because they sense it. The water is fouled because the underground springs feed from sources that pass beneath the temple's roots. The missing villagers were taken because the summoner needs them.

If the summoning completes, the demon arrives. If the demon arrives in this region, the corruption stops being a slow bleed and starts being a wildfire.

## The Factions

### The Wood Elves of the Aldwud

A band of perhaps eight wood elves under a tracker named **Sylwen Greenwatch** have noticed the corruption longer than the villagers have. They are conducting their own investigation, moving through the wood, asking questions of the trees and the older spirits there. They are not the cause of the corruption — they are *responding* to it.

The villagers of Holmlette have noticed the wood elves' presence and are suspicious. Wood elves are associated with the Aldwud, and the Aldwud is the source of their troubles, so a child's logic links them. Sylwen knows this and does not particularly care to correct it; her people answer to the wood, not to villages. She is not unfriendly, but she is curt.

She is a potential ally for the party. Convincing her of this will require demonstrating that the party is competent and not going to make her job harder.

### Mother Brackenwell, the Hag of the Aldwud

A green hag has lived in the Aldwud for several hundred years. The villagers call her **Mother Brackenwell** when they speak of her, which is rarely, and she has occasionally traded with isolated woodsmen for things — a year of sound sleep, a memory of someone now dead, a tooth — that she charges her own prices for.

Brackenwell is not the cause of the corruption. She is, in fact, *annoyed* by it; the bleed is upsetting her wood, frightening the things she eats, and is now close enough to her bog that she can taste it. She would not save Holmlette out of kindness. She might help the party for an unsavory price, or simply to remove a nuisance. She is a dangerous, untrustworthy potential ally — exactly the kind of figure who tests a young party's compromises.

### The Summoner

The actual antagonist is the person who found the temple and is performing the summoning. Pick the option that best serves the party makeup:

**Option A — Brother Edrim, fallen acolyte of Holmlette's church.** Father Beldwin's predecessor or assistant — a clergyman who lost his faith years ago after a personal tragedy and has been quietly studying forbidden texts ever since. Found the temple by accident. The corruption tracks back to someone the villagers thought they knew. Emotionally heavy.

**Option B — A traveling scholar or "antiquarian"** who arrived in the region six months ago, claiming to study ruins. Took rooms in Aldwud Keep. Knew about the temple before he arrived. Less personal stakes for the villagers but cleaner mystery; gives the party a clear external villain to investigate.

**Option C — A grieving noble** — perhaps Lord Tolwen's younger brother, or a knight of his hold, who lost a wife or child and made a desperate bargain in the wood. Ties the villain to the local power structure and complicates politics if the party tries to bring them to justice.

**Option D — Tied to a PC's backstory.** Hold the slot open until session zero. Could be a former mentor, sibling, or rival of one of the PCs. Strongest option for player buy-in if a backstory fits.

Recommended: Option A or D for the strongest emotional arc. Option B is the easiest to run.

## Suggested Arc Structure

**Session 1 — Setup.** Players gather in Holmlette. The troubles are introduced. Whether they're locals, hires of Lord Tolwen, an order of paladins responding to rumors, or some mix — the hook is the same: someone needs to look into what's happening at the wood's edge.

**Sessions 2–3 — Investigation.** Tainted wells. A crazed beast attack. Tracking missing villagers to the wood's edge. First encounter with the wood elves (suspicious, then guarded, then potentially helpful). Whispers of the hag.

**Session 4 — The Hag.** Direct or indirect encounter with Mother Brackenwell. She points the party toward the deep wood — or makes them earn the pointing. Establishes that something far worse than her is the actual problem.

**Sessions 5–6 — The Temple.** Finding the temple. Realizing what it is. The summoner is close to completion. Confrontation. The summoning is interrupted in some way — either prevented entirely (good ending), partially completed (a wounded demon escapes, future arc), or the demon arrives and the party survives by the skin of their teeth (campaign-defining moment).

## The Demon Itself

Choose based on party level when they reach the temple climax. The summoner's ritual can be calibrated to call something appropriate — an interrupted summoning may produce a lesser fiend; a completed one calls something the party should fear. The temple's residual hallowed energy can be a tool against the demon if the party is clever, which is a good reward for investigation and roleplay.

## Threads Forward

Even after this arc, several threads remain open for future campaigns:

- **What ended the past age?** The temple is one of countless ruins from that time. The party has now seen what's buried under their region.
- **Why are aasimar and tieflings appearing again?** The bleed from this temple is one event. Are there others? Is *something* trying to come back?
- **Mother Brackenwell** is still in the wood, and she remembers favors.
- **The wood elves** now know the party. That door is open or closed depending on how it ended.
- **Whatever was in the temple originally** — the celestial power it was dedicated to — is still nameless. Someone, somewhere, knows what it was.
