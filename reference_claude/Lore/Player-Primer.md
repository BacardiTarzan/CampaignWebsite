# A Primer for Players: Aldermark

*Everything below is what your character would know growing up in or near the village of Holmlette. For your character's species, see the individual species files. For class and rules questions, see the SRD.*

---

## The World You Live In

You live in Aldermark — a region of small villages, feudal holds, deep forests, and high mountains. It is medieval and grounded: most folk live and die within a day's walk of where they were born, the bread is hard, the winters are long, and the gods are believed in mostly because the priest believes in them.

It is also a young age.

The current age of Aldermark is recent. Centuries — but only centuries — separate now from a time when this world was very different, and very much louder. In earlier ages, beings of the Upper and Lower Planes walked Aldermark openly. Dragons flew where they pleased. Civilizations rose tall enough to leave ruins still scattered across the land — broken towers half-buried in farmland, catacombs beneath country churches, statues whose meanings have been forgotten.

That age ended. How, exactly, no one alive can say with certainty. Some elves were children then, but their memories are blurred by time. The dwarves keep records, but the records grow patchy and modern feuds tend to take priority over ancient lore. The High Elves know more than they share. Only ancient dragons truly remember, and dragons are not in the habit of explaining themselves.

For most people, the past age is a fog of song, ruin, and rumor. What's real and what's myth cannot be reliably separated. Every so often, a farmer's plow turns up a relic that should not exist; a priest finds his church was built atop something older; a child playing in a creek finds the still-warm bones of an infernal corpse.

## Magic and the Gods

Magic is uncommon here. You may have heard stories of a hedge witch in the next valley, of a wandering wizard who passed through three towns over, of a holy man who once cured a fever with a touch — but most folk go their whole lives without seeing real spellcraft. In other lands, magic is more present. Here, it is rare enough that working it openly draws stares and whispers.

The gods are real, but distant. Even in the past age, they did not walk Aldermark in person — their angels and their fiends did, on their behalf. Now those visits are vanishingly rare. Your village priest knows the gods of his church. He may not know there are Upper and Lower Planes, or what they really are. Most people don't.

## The Region

Three features dominate the area you call home:

**The Odmar Spine** — the great mountain range to the north. The dwarven kingdom is somewhere in those peaks; legends speak of older dwarven kingdoms now lost — the **Forsaken Halls** — but no one knows what really happened to them.

**The Aldwud** — the vast, deep forest stretching south and east of the Spine. Wood elves are said to live there. So are stranger things. Nobody who lives near the wood goes far into it.

**The Holds** — the patchwork of human feudal territories spread across the lowlands between the Spine and the Aldwud. Each hold is ruled by a local lord with greater or lesser claims to legitimacy. Lords feud with their neighbors over a stretch of grazing land they will all be forgotten by within three generations. Cities exist further afield, but not here.

## Holmlette

Holmlette is a small farming village of perhaps two hundred souls, set on the western edge of the Aldwud. It sits within the protectorate of Aldwud Keep, half a day's ride to the north, and pays its dues in grain, timber, and pelts.

It is not a remarkable village. It has a stone church, a wooden inn, a smithy, a mill on the small river that runs through it, and a stretch of fenced fields that have fed the same families for generations. The dirt road that runs through the village center continues north to the Keep and east into the trees, where it is mostly used by woodcutters who do not go far in.

Holmlette is rarely troubled by other holds. It is too small, too poor, and too quiet to interest its neighbors. What threats it faces come from the Aldwud — wolves in winter, the occasional bear, more rarely a beast of stranger description. The villagers know to stay clear of the deeper wood, and the deeper wood, mostly, has been content to stay clear of them.

That has been changing.

### People of Note

These are the names anyone who lives in or near Holmlette would recognize:

**Reeve Aldric** — Holmlette's reeve, appointed by Lord Tolwen to manage the village in his name. A practical man in his fifties, gray at the temples, more competent than charismatic. Knows everyone's business and most of their debts.

**Father Beldwin** — The village priest, tending the small stone church at the village center. Honest and pious, but a country cleric — his theology is simpler than a city scholar's, and his answers to hard questions tend to be "we trust the gods."

**Old Maeve** — The village's herbalist and wise woman. Lives in a cottage at the village's south edge with too many cats. Knows which leaves treat a fever, which mushrooms kill a man, and most of the older stories no one else bothers to remember. Children love her; some of their parents are not sure how they feel.

**Gertha** — Owner of the Holmlette Inn, the village's only gathering hall. Loud, quick-tempered, generous with portions, and the unofficial center of village gossip. Knows what every traveler said about every other traveler going back a decade.

**Tomas** — The village smith. Quiet, broad-shouldered, drinks more than he should. His brother went into the Aldwud last summer and did not come back. He has not been the same since.

**Wynna** — A young huntress who works the edges of the Aldwud, providing the village with venison, hides, and word of what she sees out there. The closest thing Holmlette has to a forest scout. She has been worried lately, and saying so.

### Daily Life

Holmlette runs on the seasons. Spring and autumn are planting and harvest. Summer is the long work of weeding, herding, and trading at the larger market a day's walk west. Winter is huddled around hearths, telling stories, and praying the snowfall doesn't break the roof. The Aldwud provides timber, game, and herbs. The river provides fish and water for the mill.

The village owns one priest, one smithy, one inn, one mill, one dozen militia (in name; in practice perhaps four men who can hold a spear and one sergeant who can shout), and a great deal of patience.

## The Aldwud (What People Say)

The Aldwud is the great forest east of the holds, stretching for many days' ride across hills, valleys, and one shallow river system. It is old. It was old before the current age began.

Most travelers see only the Aldwud's edge. The road from Aldwud Keep skirts the wood; the woodcutters of Holmlette work the outer trees and do not go far in. Past the first mile or two, the trees thicken, the paths fade, and the silence becomes the sort that pays attention. Few people who go deep into the Aldwud come back with stories that match each other.

What ordinary folk in the surrounding holds will tell you:

* Wolves and bears live there, as expected. Other things live there too, and what those things are depends on who you ask.
* A band of wood elves keeps a presence somewhere in the wood. They trade in furs, herbs, and tracking work with the surrounding holds. They are uncommon but not unknown — most villages have seen them once or twice.
* An old hag is said to live somewhere in the deep wood. She has lived there for a long time. The stories about her vary; the agreement is that she is not someone you want to find by accident.
* There are ruins in there. Old foundations swallowed by moss. A toppled stone here, a half-buried arch there. Some are said to be older than the age — older even than the dwarven holds. The villagers do not visit them.

Past the first few miles in, what's known runs out. Game trails fade into nothing. The light goes wrong. There are stories — unmatched, contradictory, told late at night — about old stones with markings that no priest can read, about silences that listen, about places no woodcutter has ever returned from. None of these stories agree. Most of them mention the same kind of feeling.

The wood does not surrender its secrets easily. It has had a very long time to keep them.

### Aldwud Keep

The Keep itself sits at the western edge of the wood, half a day's ride north of Holmlette. It is a modest stone keep with an attached village of a few hundred — a market hall, a small garrison, two churches, a craftsmen's quarter.

**Lord Tolwen** holds Aldwud Keep, swearing allegiance upward to a regional baron whose name Holmlette villagers occasionally remember. Tolwen is in his early forties, a careful administrator more than a warrior, and rules his hold reasonably well by the standards of the region.

## Recent Troubles

In the past few weeks, things have begun going wrong in and around Holmlette:

**The water has soured.** Buckets drawn from certain wells now smell faintly off. A child fell ill after drinking from the river; a goat died after the same. Father Beldwin has blessed the wells but the trouble has not stopped.

**The animals have grown strange.** Hunters report deer that did not flee, and once a fox that did not seem to see them at all. Wynna saw a wolf with sores on its flanks that should have killed it and hadn't.

**People have gone missing.** Two woodcutters and a young girl who was gathering mushrooms at the wood's edge. None have returned.

Reeve Aldric is worried. Father Beldwin has begun praying nightly. Old Maeve has stopped saying anything about it at all, which some find more troubling than if she had spoken up.

There is a feeling, in Holmlette, that something is wrong in the Aldwud. No one yet knows what.

## A Note for Your Backstory

You don't have to be from Holmlette to start the campaign here, but if you are, you'll have a head start on knowing the people listed above and the rhythms of village life. If you're from elsewhere, think about what brought you to this corner of Aldermark — a calling, a contract, a relative, a road you were told not to take. Whatever it is, you're here now, and something in the wood is starting to stir.
