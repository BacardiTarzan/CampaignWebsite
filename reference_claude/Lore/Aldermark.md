# Aldermark

Aldermark is a region of small villages, feudal holds, deep forests, and high mountains, set on the Material Plane. It is medieval and grounded — most folk live and die within a day's walk of where they were born, the bread is hard, the winters are long, and the gods are believed in mostly because the priest believes in them.

It is also young.

## The Young Age

The current age of Aldermark is recent. Centuries — but only centuries — separate now from a time when this world was very different, and very much louder. In earlier ages, beings of the Upper and Lower Planes walked Aldermark openly. Dragons flew where they pleased. In the last age, civilizations rose tall enough to leave ruins still scattered across the land — broken towers half-buried in farmland, catacombs beneath country churches, statues whose meanings have been forgotten.

That age ended. How, exactly, no one alive can say with certainty. Some elves were children then; the dwarves keep records but the records grow patchy and modern feuds gain priority over ancient lore; the High Elves know more than they share. Only ancient dragons truly remember, and dragons are not in the habit of explaining themselves.

For the people of Aldermark, the past age is a fog of song, ruin, and rumor. What's real and what's myth cannot be reliably separated. Every so often, a farmer's plow turns up a relic that should not exist; a priest finds his church was built atop something older; a child playing in a creek finds the still-warm bones of an infernal corpse.

## Geography

The region of Aldermark is dominated by three features:

* **The Odmar Spine** — the great mountain range to the north, home to the dwarven kingdom and the source of most legends about the Forsaken Halls.
* **The Aldwud** — a vast, deep forest stretching south and east of the Spine. Home to wood elves, scattered hermits, and things older than the current age.
* **The Holds** — the patchwork of human feudal territories that spread across the lowlands, valleys, and farmland between the Spine and the Aldwud. Each is ruled by a local lord with greater or lesser claims to legitimacy.

Cities exist further afield. The starting region is not where the great courts and universities of Aldermark are found.

## The Cosmology

Aldermark sits on the Material Plane within the broader multiverse — but most people in this region have only the haziest notion of any of that. A village priest knows their gods. A scholar might know there are Upper and Lower Planes. A High Elf knows quite a bit more. A dragon knows almost everything, and tells almost nothing.

The gods are real. They are also distant, and always have been. Even in the past age, they did not walk Aldermark in person — their angels and their fiends did, on their behalf. Now, in the new age, even those visits are vanishingly rare.

## Magic

Magic in this region is uncommon. A traveling adventurer will hear stories of a hedge witch in the next valley, of a wandering wizard who passed through three towns over, of a holy man who once cured a fever with a touch — but most folk go their whole lives without seeing real spellcraft. In the wider world, magic is more present. In other lands, much more present. Here, in this corner of the world known as Aldermark, it is rare enough that working it openly draws stares and whispers.
